# Data

## Summary

No training data was supplied, and the service does not train anything.
I built a **synthetic invoice generator** to create test PDFs with known
correct answers, and used them only to **measure** the rule-based extractor.

| Test set | Invoices | Purpose | Score (all 8 fields) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Placeholder baseline | 60 | Extractor that returns empty fields | 6.0% |
| Group A | 300 | Used while building | 100.0% |
| Group B, blind | 300 | Held out; never looked at while building | **75.6%** |
| Group B, after fixes | 300 | Same set, after fixing general weaknesses B revealed | 98.9% |
| Hand-made Word invoices | 2 | Made outside the generator, answers written first | 16 / 16 fields |

The **blind B score (75.6%) is my best estimate** for unseen invoices.
The 98.9% is optimistic, because those fixes were guided by B (details below).

## Where the data came from

Everything is generated by `data_gen/generate.py` from:

- **Appendix M**: the six vendors, their countries and currencies, and the
  day/month rule.
- **Appendix N**: the two example invoices (also rebuilt exactly as test fixtures).
- **Appendix O**: the business rules. Each rule has its own scenario.
- My own knowledge of real invoices (UK VAT, US sales tax, German MwSt,
  Japanese 消費税, Indian CGST/SGST, typical layouts, typical noise).

## How an invoice is made

Each invoice randomly combines:

- **Vendor**: one of the six, printed in a varied way (upper case, with or
  without Ltd/Inc/GmbH/KK, British/American spelling, typos), or an unknown vendor.
- **Layout**: four layouts (label beside value, label above value, two columns
  on one line, compact lines with no title).
- **Labels**: English, plus German and Japanese for those vendors.
- **Dates**: each country's convention; about 40% fall on days 1–12, so they
  are genuinely ambiguous. Formats include 03/09/2026, 9/3/26, 03.09.2026,
  2026年9月3日, 3 September 2026, Sep 3, 2026 and dates with weekdays.
- **Numbers**: 1,234.56 / 1234.56 / 1.234,56 / 1 234,56 / 1,23,456.00 / ¥12,000.
- **Decoys**: PO numbers, customer numbers and order dates that look like answers.

### Business rules (Appendix O)

| Rule | How the generator tests it |
|---|---|
| O1 | Due date only, terms only, both agreeing, both **disagreeing**, or neither |
| O2 | 6% of invoices have a printed total that does not add up |
| O3 | Some invoices print no currency at all |
| O4 | 8% are quotations, delivery notes or statements |
| O5 | All JPY amounts are printed without decimals |

### Extra real-world anomalies

Footers with bank and phone numbers, a second "Ship to" address, upper-case
labels, labels without colons, labels with dot leaders, currency on every
amount, rotated PAID/COPY watermarks, multi-page invoices with totals on
page 2, zero-rated tax, Indian round-off lines, "amount in words" lines and
full-width Japanese digits. Odds are in `CHANCES` in `generate.py`.

The correct answer for each invoice is written **from the brief's rules**:
non-invoices have every field null; disagreeing due dates use the printed
date (O1); wrong totals keep the printed value (O2); missing currency uses
the vendor's currency (O3).

### Reproducibility

Seeds are fixed: 1 for group A and 2 for group B. The same commands give the
same PDFs (reportlab's `invariant` mode). Counts for each scenario, layout,
country and anomaly are saved in `eval/results/generate_A.txt` and
`generate_B.txt`.

## Groups A and B: testing on unseen wording

The brief says the hidden invoices use layouts, labels and date formats
"you have not seen". Because I wrote both the generator and the extractor,
testing on the same wording I built with would prove nothing. So every list
in `vocab.py` is split:

- **Group A** (layouts: label beside value, label above value): used for building.
- **Group B** (layouts: two columns, compact): different labels, layouts,
  number styles and vendor spellings. Not used for building.

Rule: nothing from group B was copied into `app/labels.py` (checked with
`grep` before measuring).

One overlap exists: `vendors.py` skips text containing "Customer" or
"Invoice to" so the buyer is never mistaken for the vendor. These are also
two of group B's "Bill to" labels. They were in the code from the first
version (general invoice knowledge, not copied from B) and only affect
vendor matching, which scored 100% in blind B.

## How the data was used to test

1. **`eval/evaluate.py`** scores exact match per field after normalisation,
   the same metric as the brief (dates as ISO, amounts to two decimals,
   vendor as canonical name). It also times every request and can break
   scores down by layout, country, label or anomaly.
2. **`tests/`** (79 tests, no network): unit tests per module, one test per
   business rule, the two Appendix N examples rebuilt as PDFs and checked
   against the brief's expected output, the HTTP endpoints, ten generated
   invoices (one per rule or anomaly) and two hand-made Word invoices, all
   saved in `tests/fixtures/`. Every bug found during development has a
   regression test.

## Results

### Group B, blind (`eval/results/B.txt`)

| vendor | number | inv. date | due date | currency | subtotal | tax | total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 100% | 48.0% | 88.7% | 58.3% | 88.7% | 74.3% | 68.3% | 78.3% |

### What B revealed, and what I changed

I looked at the B breakdowns, found causes with evidence (for example, every
wrong "total" was exactly subtotal × (1 + tax rate), which proved we were
returning the subtotal), and fixed each with a **general rule**, not by
copying B labels:

1. **Non-invoice titles** in German and Japanese and short forms ("quote").
   The 34 B non-invoices were missed, costing 34 points on 7 fields.
2. **Fallback search for invoice numbers** now also looks in the next segment
   and the line below. The two-column layout went from 10% to 96%.
3. **Due dates without a known label**: a date next to a word starting with
   pay/due/fällig.
4. **Dotted acronyms** (V.A.T.) and German compound words (Umsatzsteuer).
5. **A "total" followed by tax and a bigger amount is the subtotal**
   (e.g. "Total before tax").
6. **Table header "Particulars | Total"** was not recognised.

Two group A bugs were also fixed: a vendor name sharing a line with
"Delivery Note" was skipped (vendor 99.0% → 100%).

| | Overall | number | due date | subtotal | tax | total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| B blind | 75.6% | 48.0% | 58.3% | 74.3% | 68.3% | 78.3% |
| B after fixes | **98.9%** | 94.7% | 98.0% | 99.3% | 99.0% | 100% |

**After these fixes, group B is no longer blind.** The 98.9% is an upper
bound, not a prediction.

### Speed

About 50 ms per invoice on average, slowest about 220 ms, on a laptop CPU
(limit: 1 second). PDFs are capped at 20 pages to keep this true.

## Hand-made invoices (not from the generator)

To test beyond my own generator, I typed two invoices in Microsoft Word,
wrote down the correct answers first, saved them as PDF and ran them.
They use a different PDF producer (Word tables and fonts) and labels the
extractor does not list ("Document number", "Date of invoice",
"Payment: within 21 days", "In No:").

| Invoice | Fields correct | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Meridian Industrial (German formats, EUR) | 8 / 8 | Invoice number found by the general fallback, confidence 0.6 |
| Northwind Traders (total printed as 43) | 8 / 8 | Rule O2: printed total returned, mismatch warning given |

Both are kept in `tests/fixtures/` and checked by `tests/test_handmade.py`.
Two invoices are not a statistic, but they show the rules work on PDFs
made by a different tool, with wording I did not list.

## What I expect to break on real data

Both groups were written by me, so anything I did not imagine is missing
from both. I expect the real score to be **lower than B**. Likely problems:

- **Labels nobody listed**, especially if the value is not beside or directly
  below the label.
- **Real PDF layouts**: tables with borders, text stored out of reading order
  by design tools, values in table cells far from their labels.
- **Several dates or reference numbers** in the header with unfamiliar labels.
- **Non-invoices in other languages** or with unusual titles.
- **Amounts after the total** (discounts, amounts already paid, balance carried).
- **Real typography**: ligatures, unusual fonts, the ₹ symbol (not used in the
  generator because standard PDF fonts lack it).
- Everything visual is idealised: one font, no logos, no scans.
- **Rule O3 on unseen wording**: group B always prints a currency, so the
  "no currency" rule was measured only on group A (112 invoices, all correct)
  and in the unit and fixture tests.
